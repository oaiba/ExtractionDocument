    Environmental Narrative Guidelines: “Show, Don’t Tell”#

    ← Back to Index | Visual Style

    🏛️ The Silent Storyteller#

    Our world, Aethelgard, is dead. There are no safe zones for NPCs to give quests. The environment must be the primary method of delivering lore and plot.

    Core Principle: “The Aftermath”#

    Every location should look like something happened there right before the player arrived. Avoid static, perfect presets.

    🔍 Narrative Layers#

    Layer 1: The Panic (Immediate Past)#

    Shows the moment society collapsed.

    • Asset: Suitcases spilled open on a highway.
    • Implication: People fled in a hurry.
    • Asset: Half-eaten meals on a restaurant table.
    • Implication: The disaster struck instantly, unexpectedly.
    • Asset: Cars crashed into barricades, not just parked.
    • Implication: Drivers were desperate or terrified.

    Layer 2: The Struggle (Recent Past)#

    Shows survivors trying to live in the ruins.

    • Asset: A mattress in a bank vault.
    • Implication: Someone thought money would keep them safe, or the thick walls were shelter.
    • Asset: Rain collectors made of tarps.
    • Implication: Water systems failed; desperation set in.
    • Asset: Makeshift graves in a playground.
    • Implication: High casualties; no space or time for proper burial.

    Layer 3: The Decay (Present)#

    Shows nature reclaiming the world.

    • Asset: Trees growing through office floors.
    • Implication: It’s been years, not days.
    • Asset: Rust streaks dripping down concrete.
    • Implication: Maintenance stopped long ago.
    • Asset: Animals nesting in vehicle engines.
    • Implication: Wildlife no longer fears machinery.

    🖼️ “Micro-Stories” (Vignettes)#

    Create small, self-contained scenes (dioramas) within the larger level.

    Example A: The Last Stand#

    • Setting: A bathroom stall in a subway station.
    • Props: A skeleton clutching a revolver. One spent casing on the floor. Door barricaded from the inside.
    • Story: A survivor hid here, heard the monsters coming, realized escape was impossible, and took their own way out. NO TEXT LOG NEEDED.

    Example B: The Deal Gone Wrong#

    • Setting: An alleyway behind a pharmacy.
    • Props: Two skeletons facing each other. A duffel bag of meds between them. Both have knives in their chests.
    • Story: A trade turned violent. Greed killed both parties. The loot (meds) is still there for the player.

    Example C: The Hopeless Signal#

    • Setting: A rooftop with a generator and radio tower.
    • Props: “SOS” painted in white on the roof. The generator is out of fuel. A skeleton sits by the radio, headphones on.
    • Story: Someone waited for rescue that never came.

    📦 Loot Logic (Diegetic Placement)#

    Loot must make sense in its context. This rewards players who read the environment.

    • Medical Loot: DO NOT put medkits in random wooden crates. Put them in:
      • Ambulances
      • Bathrooms (cabinets)
      • Triage tents
      • Offices (First Aid wall boxes)
    • Weapon Parts: found on workbenches, gun store ruins, police trunks.
    • Food: Kitchens, supermarkets, vending machines (broken).

    🔦 Lighting as Narrative#

    Use lighting to highlight these stories, but also to set the emotional tone.

    • Red Light: Danger, emergency, “Do Not Enter”. (Naturally attracts players due to curiosity).
    • Green Light: Safety, medical, “Exit”.
    • Flickering Light: Instability, recent damage, tension. (e.g., A light flickering near a vent suggests something is moving inside the vent).

    🚫 Narrative Anti-Patterns#

    1. Written Notes everywhere: Avoid “My Dearest Diary…” pages scattered in illogical places. Use environmental clues first.
    2. Blood without a Body: If there is a massive blood trail, it must lead somewhere (a body, a drag mark, a monster den). It cannot just end at a wall.
    3. Perfect Skeletons: Skeletons should be disarticulated, scavenged by animals, or crushed. Avoid the “Halloween Prop” look.
